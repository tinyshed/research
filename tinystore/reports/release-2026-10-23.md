# The first release against what it replaces — 2026-10-23

> The idle round was run again on `v0.4.0` in
> [at rest, 2026-10-27](at-rest-2026-10-27.md): the server holds 16.7 MiB
> there, the five services as here.

TinyStore `v0.1.0` as npm and crates.io publish it, beside the services a
small application runs without it: PostgreSQL 18, Redis 8.10 (and Valkey
9.1), MinIO, VictoriaMetrics 1.154 (and Prometheus 3.15) and Loki 3.7. One
machine, one stack at a time, medians of three passes for the product rounds
and two for the archive rounds. The cards in TinyStore's README and on its
site are drawn from this round's data.

At rest, TinyStore's server with every engine open holds 14 MiB where the five
services hold 402, and it answers 0.13 s after it starts where Loki takes
15.5. An application on it, driven from Bun, answers 1.7 times the requests
of the same application on the five services at 64 clients and 2.2 times at
256, with a quarter of the memory; at one and eight clients the services
answer 2.2 to 2.6 times more, because a request that writes waits for two
syncs, one per file, where PostgreSQL's transaction waits for one. Point reads
in the program's process take 6 µs where Redis takes 43 and PostgreSQL 81;
writes that return before the disk sync stop at 30,000 a second where Redis
reaches 200,000. On TSBS DevOps TinyStore keeps a sample in 1.00 bytes where
VictoriaMetrics keeps one in 0.71 and Prometheus in 1.58, with 15 MiB of
memory where they hold 258 and 104; on 13 LogHub datasets it keeps 14.2 % of
the text where Loki keeps 19.3 % and zstd -19 5.5 %, with 12 to 21 MiB where
Loki holds 219 to 1,285. It ingests 1.4 million samples a second against
VictoriaMetrics' 2.1, and 0.8 to 2.2 million lines against Loki's 0.14 to 0.28.
The round fixed four things in TinyStore and filed ten issues.

## Metrics: TSBS DevOps against VictoriaMetrics and Prometheus

The same samples reach every contender in the same calls: ten minutes of the
corpus at a time, cut into calls of at most 10,000 samples. TinyStore's core
takes them in a Rust program's process, VictoriaMetrics as Prometheus' text
with timestamps over HTTP, and Prometheus, which takes no old samples but
through its backfill, from promtool's blocks built from the same samples
written as OpenMetrics. A query is TSBS's, asked of each in its own words:
TinyStore's `aggregate` and `read`, PromQL over HTTP for the others, timed to
the answer's last byte, JSON included for the two services.

| Corpus           | Contender       | On disk, MiB | Bytes a sample | Of which samples' blocks       | Ingest, samples/s | CPU to ingest, s | Memory at ingest, MiB | Memory settled, MiB |

| wide, 5,090,400  | TinyStore       | 7.3          | 1.50           | 0.68                           | 1.48 M            | 4.5              | 17.6                  | 16.5                |
|                  | VictoriaMetrics | 5.9          | 1.22           | 0.63 merged, before its caches | 2.15 M            | 6.3              | 325                   | 272                 |

| long, 26,179,200 | TinyStore       | 24.9         | 1.00           | 0.70                           | 1.43 M            | 23.2             | 14.7                  | 14.7, at ingest     |
|                  | VictoriaMetrics | 17.8         | 0.71           | 0.63 merged, before its caches | 2.09 M            | 34.0             | 404                   | 258                 |

VictoriaMetrics keeps every sample in less than TinyStore does, and the gap is
the file's more than the codec's: TinyStore's blocks of samples take 0.68 and
0.70 bytes a sample against VictoriaMetrics' 0.63 merged parts, 8 to 11 % more.
The rest of TinyStore's file is SQLite's: `dbstat` divides the long corpus's
24.9 MiB into 20.2 MiB of the samples' pages (18.4 MiB of payload in them),
3.9 MiB of the block directory, `groups`, and 2 MiB of heads, postings, labels
and the rest. On the wide corpus the heads take 1.9 MiB of pages holding 0.3
MiB: a head is rewritten as its series grows, and the pages it left stay free
in the file, which SQLite reuses rather than gives back. A longer series pays
the directory and the heads over more samples, which is the step from 1.50 to
1.00.

services, whose range is left-open; every grouped query the same buckets.

pages given back.

## What the round fixed, and what it filed

Fixed in TinyStore on `main`, each with a test where one fits:

- **A logger's flush in pieces.** Bun's, Python's and Go's loggers sent their
  whole buffer as one `records.append`; past 2 MiB of lines the request was
  refused and every line in it dropped. The app round's first try lost lines
  so, with a buffer of 65,536.
- **Bun's loop asleep while the core works.** With no timer armed, Bun 1.4.2
  spins at a whole processor while it waits for a threadsafe callback from
  another thread: a lone synced writer in process used 1.03 processors, and
  0.07 with a far-off timer armed while the store is open. A ten-line cdylib
  calling a threadsafe callback every 3 ms reproduces it without TinyStore:
  1.00 processor without the timer, 0.02 with it.
- **`fdatasync` in the release.** `tools/native.sh` now sets
  `-DHAVE_FDATASYNC=1` for every Linux target, as CI did. With it a lone
  durable commit takes 1.69–1.74 ms against 3.05–3.17 ms with `fsync`, three
  runs of 2000 each, which is PostgreSQL's 1.78; strace counted 3,199 `fsync`
  and no `fdatasync` from the published library.
- **The default durability, as the docs say it.** The Bun, Python and Go
  documentation and CONTRACTS' DUR-01 said `'full'` is every file's default;
  records and metrics commit `'os'` unless told.

## What was measured, and why this way

The round asks what a developer who runs PostgreSQL, Redis and S3 today gets
from TinyStore instead: how much the stack costs at rest, how an application
on it holds up under load, and where each engine stands against the service
it replaces. Logs and metrics are measured twice, once for the product and
once on the core itself for the archive question: what each keeps on disk,
how fast it takes it, and how fast it answers from it.

| Round     | What a case does                                                                                                                                 | Contenders                                                                                                           |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| idle      | opens every engine a small application uses, writes each once, then rests 60 s                                                                   | Bun alone; TinyStore in process, its sidecar, `tinystore serve`; the five services; Valkey and Prometheus each alone |
| app       | a request reads a session and a note, logs a line, counts itself; 1 in 10 updates the note and queues a job; 1 in 50 uploads 16 KiB              | TinyStore in process, in process with the queue in the note's database, its sidecar; the five services               |
| kv        | 128-byte values over 100,000 keys, reads once, writes twice: synced (each write on the disk) and written (on the operating system, synced later) | TinyStore in process and sidecar; Redis 8.10 and Valkey 9.1                                                          |
| kv-memory | a million 128-byte values written, a rest of 20 s, reads at 64 in flight                                                                         | as kv, each as it ships                                                                                              |
| sql       | a row read by its primary key and a row inserted, over 100,000                                                                                   | TinyStore in process and sidecar; PostgreSQL 18                                                                      |
| files     | 16 KiB and 1 MiB files read whole and replaced whole                                                                                             | TinyStore in process and sidecar; MinIO's last release                                                               |
| metrics   | TSBS DevOps ingested in the same batches, settled, then TSBS' queries                                                                            | TinyStore's core in a Rust program; VictoriaMetrics 1.154; Prometheus 3.15, backfilled by promtool                   |
| logs      | each of 13 LogHub datasets with the times its own lines spell, in batches of 1000, then five reads                                               | TinyStore's core in a Rust program; Loki 3.7                                                                         |

Every program but the archive's is a Bun program: Bun is the SDK most of
TinyStore's users start from, and Bun ships its own clients for Redis,
PostgreSQL and S3, written natively, so the services are reached by the
fastest client a Bun program has. Both sides pay the same JavaScript a call.
The numbers say what an application in Bun gets, not what each engine can do
at most: the core's own ceiling is in the archive rounds and in the
[slice round](rust-slice-2026-10-10.md).

TinyStore is v0.1.0 as the registries publish it: `@tinyshed/tinystore@0.1.0`
from npm, with its Linux library and binary, for every Bun program, and the
crate `tinystore = "=0.1.0"` from crates.io, built with the SQLite flags of
TinyStore's CI, for the archive's. Nothing was built from a checkout.

## Durability, said the same on both sides

A write is compared only with a write that promises the same. Two promises are
measured where a service offers both:

| Promise                                         | TinyStore                                  | Redis and Valkey                         | PostgreSQL                           | MinIO        |
|-------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------|
| synced: on the disk before the call returns     | `'full'`, the default of kv, sql and files | `appendonly yes`, `appendfsync always`   | `synchronous_commit on`, its default | its default  |
| written: survives the process, not a power loss | `'os'`, synced at the WAL's checkpoint     | `appendonly yes`, `appendfsync everysec` | not measured                         | not measured |

The second row is the same promise to a crashed process, not the same window
to a power loss: Redis syncs its file every second, TinyStore at the WAL's
next checkpoint, which may be later. Redis's own default, snapshots and no
append-only file, promises less than either and is not compared with a write
that waits for anything. VictoriaMetrics answers an ingest before its samples
are on the disk, and Loki before its chunks are, both by design: the archive
rounds time an ingest to its answer on every side and say so.

## Settings changed, and why

Each service runs as its vendor ships it, with a change only where the round
needs the same promise from both sides or where the service would refuse the
round's load:

| Service         | Changed                                                                                                           | Why                                                                            |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| PostgreSQL      | `max_connections` 100 → 200; a Unix socket, no TCP                                                                | a pool of 64, its worker and the next run's pool                               |
| Redis, Valkey   | `appendonly`, `appendfsync` as the row above says; a Unix socket                                                  | the promise compared                                                           |
| MinIO           | the console and the update check off                                                                              | nothing the round reads                                                        |
| VictoriaMetrics | `-search.disableCache`                                                                                            | a repeated query answered from its samples, as the others answer it            |
| Prometheus      | `--web.enable-remote-write-receiver` in the product rounds                                                        | the samples arrive as an agent sends them                                      |
| Loki            | ingestion rate and burst raised; for LogHub, old samples taken, no query length bound, chunks flushed at shutdown | the round's rate measured rather than refused; LogHub's dates are 2005 to 2017 |
| TinyStore       | a stream's `keep` of 40 years for LogHub; the logger's buffer 8192 lines in the app round                         | LogHub's dates; a burst of 50,000 lines a second kept rather than dropped      |

TinyStore's logger keeps 1024 lines by default and drops what a full buffer
has no room for, as a logger that never waits must. The app round raises it
so that both sides keep every line, which the round checks.

## Memory, counted one way for everyone

Every case runs in one container, the services as its child processes, one
stack at a time. Ten times a second the harness reads each process's
proportional set size from `/proc/<pid>/smaps_rollup` and sums it by group: a
service and every process under it, the Bun program, and TinyStore's server,
counted apart wherever it hangs. PSS divides a page shared by several
processes among them, so PostgreSQL's backends and Redis's snapshot child are
counted once, not once a process. Beside it the harness reads the container's
cgroup, whose `file` is the page cache every process brought in: a store that
reads its files through the cache, as SQLite does, holds data there that PSS
leaves out, and a store that keeps its data in its own memory, as Redis does,
holds it in PSS. Both are reported; the page cache is emptied before each
case so that what it holds is the case's own.

At rest is the median of the last minute of a case that opened everything and
wrote each engine once; under load the most any sample saw.

## Corpora

A TSBS corpus is moved by whole days to end within the hour before the round,
so that every contender keeps it under its default retention; its times of
day and its spacing stay. A LogHub line's time is the one its own text spells,
read by a pattern per dataset, as a shipper parses it: a line that spells
none takes the one before it, and lines of one time take a nanosecond each
after it, so that Loki keeps two equal lines of one second as two.
