# Search at a million documents, FTS5 against Tantivy — 16 October 2026

[search-plan-2026-10-16](search-plan-2026-10-16.md) found FTS5's ranked tail
cut sixfold at 88,175 documents by leaving out the words in more than 5 % of
them, and left a million documents unmeasured. [search-2026-10-16](search-2026-10-16.md)
measured a million as its pool repeated, sixty Russian questions and an `or`
of every word. This round puts both engines on one million real passages,
every one of the 600 judged questions, the same tokens, fields, weights and
plans, each engine owning its documents: what it stores, what it reads back,
what a set costs and what it holds in memory, in the same session. Nothing
here is a product promise.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does leaving out frequent words hold at a million? | Yes: FTS5's p99 goes from 505–533 ms to 85–90 ms at the same nDCG@10, or 48–55 ms at a 2 % cut for 2.7 % less |
| How far apart are the reads? | Tantivy answers the same pruned plan at a p99 of 1.4 ms, 60–65 times sooner; its `or` at 3.7–4.3 ms, 125–136 times sooner than FTS5's |
| Is the quality the same? | Tantivy's nDCG@10 is 4 % higher (0.4353 against 0.4177) and its MRR@10 10 %; recall@10 is level |
| What does a set cost? | FTS5 3.3–3.5 ms at the median and 6.6–8.9 ms at p99; Tantivy 57–58 ms and 85–103 ms. A hundred sets a commit: 17–44 ms against 62–83 ms |
| What does a reader hold? | FTS5 10 MiB over the process's 58; Tantivy 250–265 MiB, the pages of its memory-mapped files it touched |
| What does a million take on disk? | FTS5 1,240 MiB, 666 of them the documents as written; Tantivy 706 MiB, its stored fields compressed |

## The collection

The 88,175 judged passages of Russian, English and Chinese
[NoMIRACL](https://huggingface.co/datasets/miracl/nomiracl), then 911,825
passages nobody judged from the first shard of each language's
[MIRACL corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/miracl/miracl-corpus) — the
same Wikipedias, 303,943 Russian and 303,941 each English and Chinese — a
passage the pool holds left out of the shard. Its text is 561 MiB. A passage
nobody judged counts as not relevant, so a relevant one among them lowers
both engines' quality alike.

Both engines index the analyzer of search-plan-2026-10-16, `auto`, into the
same four fields with the same weights, and own their documents: FTS5 keeps
each one's key, title and body in a table of the same file by the rowid its
postings carry, Tantivy in stored fields with its key indexed whole. Each is
built in one process, merged into one segment, and read by others. A query is
timed from its text to its ten ids, and again with the ten documents read
back. A set replaces a document by its key and returns once a reader would
see it: FTS5's commit, or Tantivy's commit and the reader's reload.

## Reads

Two rounds, FTS5 first and then Tantivy first, each process after a warm pass
of its own; 600 questions.

| | p50 ms | p99 ms | read back, p99 ms | nDCG@10 | Recall@10 | MRR@10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FTS5, `or` | 79.61; 74.14 | 504.73; 533.29 | 504.79; 533.33 | 0.4177 | 0.5662 | 0.4326 |
| FTS5, `pruned-or`, 5 % | 24.09; 23.52 | 89.97; 85.41 | 90.03; 85.46 | 0.4177 | 0.5656 | 0.4336 |
| FTS5, `pruned-or`, 2 % | 12.94; 13.18 | 48.11; 55.05 | 48.17; 55.11 | 0.4065 | 0.5541 | 0.4163 |
| Tantivy, `or` | 0.32; 0.36 | 3.72; 4.28 | 3.75; 4.35 | 0.4353 | 0.5580 | 0.4760 |
| Tantivy, `pruned-or`, 5 % | 0.22; 0.21 | 1.39; 1.42 | 1.42; 1.47 | 0.4370 | 0.5642 | 0.4755 |
| Tantivy, `pruned-or`, 2 % | 0.14; 0.14 | 0.90; 0.91 | 0.92; 0.94 | 0.4298 | 0.5538 | 0.4706 |

Reading ten documents back adds hundredths of a millisecond to either.
FTS5's time is English's above all: at a 5 % cut its round 1 p50 is 23.1 ms
for Russian questions, 42.1 ms for English and 13.1 ms for Chinese.

At 88,175 documents in search-plan-2026-10-16, a different harness without
stored documents, Tantivy's `or` was 66–74 times sooner than FTS5's at p99 and
its pruned plan 28–30 times; here 125–136 and 60–65 times. FTS5's time grew about as its documents did and Tantivy's about
half as fast: a hypothesis for why, which this round does not separate, is
that FTS5 ranks every document a kept word is in, and Tantivy's block-max WAND
skips the blocks that cannot reach its top ten.

FTS5's sets run with `synchronous=full` in WAL, as TinyStore's do, each its
own transaction rather than one of a grouped commit. Tantivy's writer has a
64 MiB budget and its default merge policy.

## Memory and disk

| | store MiB | build s | build HWM MiB | reader: before open; HWM, MiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FTS5 | 1,240: documents 666, postings 511, key index 20, sizes 16 | 96 | 727 | 58–59; 68 |
| Tantivy | 706 | 58 | 1,779 | 58–59; 309–323 |

A build's peak holds the collection's text as well. A reader holds the
questions and every document's id before it opens the index, the same in both.
FTS5 reads through SQLite's 1 MiB cache and the kernel's page cache, which no
process counts; Tantivy maps its files, and the pages it touched count in its
resident memory. FTS5's table of the 1,793 terms in more than 0.5 % of the
documents took 1.6 s to build and opens in 36–45 ms.

## What this round does not show

- An index between merges: both were merged into one segment before they were
  read, and FTS5's would otherwise hold more than one.
- Readers at once, a reader beside a writer, keyword queries, typos.
- Grouped commits: each set is a transaction of its own.
- One machine, one container; no significance test, and quality is the same
  in both rounds to the last digit.

## What follows

At a million documents the trade is now measured on both sides. FTS5 with the
statistics' stop words answers at a p50 of 13–24 ms and a p99 of 48–90 ms,
sets in 3.4 ms and reads in 10 MiB, inside the store's file, its
transactions, its backup and the replication that file will have. Tantivy
answers 60–136 times sooner with 4 % better nDCG@10, sets 17 times slower,
reads in 25 times the memory, and is another format beside the store. Which
one a standalone collection uses is the owner's decision; this round says
only what each costs. Rounds that would move it: a million between merges,
keyword queries, readers at once, and a store of blocks with their maximum
scores over SQLite pages, which would give FTS5's file WAND's skipping.

## Environment and reproduction

Linux 6.18.33.2-microsoft-standard-WSL2 in Docker Desktop's WSL2 VM on an AMD
Ryzen 7 7700, the container limited to 2 CPUs and 6 GiB; Rust 1.99.0;
rusqlite 0.40.2 with SQLite 3.53.2, opened as TinyStore opens a file (4 KiB
pages, WAL, `synchronous=full`, a 1 MiB cache); Tantivy 0.26.2. NoMIRACL at
`ecd08778d0426a5ca28ac99763b0c9ddc2c78e68` and MIRACL's corpus at
`d921ec7e349ce0d28daf30b2da9da5ee698bef0d`, checked against
[corpus.sha256](../search-plan-lab/corpus.sha256) and
[miracl.sha256](../search-plan-lab/miracl.sha256).

Harness `8950337` ran [run1m.sh](../search-plan-lab/run1m.sh) from 09:27:38
to 09:38:54 UTC. The raw lines and every question's ten passages are in
[data/search-1m-2026-10-16](data/search-1m-2026-10-16/), and
`summarize1m.py` writes the tables above from them.
