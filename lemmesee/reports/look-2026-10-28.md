## The five, learned from the teachers' fives

`choose.py` scored each of round 1c's 480 teacher schemas with a ranker
fitted on the other four folds, then tuned seven penalties, one for each
thing two panels may share, by coordinate ascent on the agreement with the
teachers, in the same folds; round 1c's rules are the starting point, a
penalty of 9 being a rule.

| | Haiku: precision | recall | Gemini: precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| round 1c's rules | 0.514 | 0.334 | 0.541 | 0.363 |
| **learned penalties** | **0.532** | **0.355** | **0.563** | **0.388** |

The penalties fitted on all: one time column shared, by the same measure or
another, 0; the same breakdown with the same measure 0.25, with another 9;
the same base and the same measure 0; one shape 0. The teachers put several
panels over one time and never one breakdown twice.

## The look

Twenty-eight databases gave a pool each: round 1c's ranker's thirty best
questions, its five and round 1d's choice, run on the database, and round
1's B1 five as it ran. Round 1c's eight — Chinook, the airlines demo,
ErgastF1, Northwind, ccs, Lahman, classicmodels, Credit — and twenty from
CTU's repository, none a pilot fixture: pubs, world, Dallas, restbase, voc,
medical, PremierLeague, Basketball_men, NCAA, Hockey, imdb_MovieLens,
Student_loan, CraftBeer, NBA, Basketball_women, Mondial, SFScores,
Shakespeare, Atherosclerosis and ftp.

The look is a model of a teaching judge's keep — LightGBM, 150 trees of
four leaves — from the result's sixteen numbers, the ranker's score and
rank, and the question's own features; for each database it is fitted on
the other twenty-seven. Its log-odds times a weight are added to the
ranker's score, then round 1d's penalties choose. The judge that taught it
never counts it.

## The judges

database returned, without its SQL, blind and shuffled, by round 1c's form
and a line more: a single bar, an empty result or a column of blanks is not
worth a chart.

## What it means, and what does not follow

- **Every judge is a model.** Five that never taught the look keep more of
  its five, four of them clear of chance, but they agree with each other
  on 65 to 91 % of panels: they are opinions of a kind, not people.
- **The ceiling is the choice.** A choice from the same candidates could
  keep 4.6 of five by Opus; the look keeps 2.7. Each doubling of the
  answers moved it, and **a teacher at 2.3 cents for 845 panels teaches
  nearly as well as Sonnet**: labels are no longer what limits it.
- **Round 1c alone did no better than round 1 here** by Sonnet, Opus and
  Gemini, and better by DeepSeek, GLM and Jev. Round 1c's own blind judging
  kept it at 0.70 and 0.60 against 0.54 and 0.49; this one is larger, with
  more judges; the clear gain over round 1 is the look's.
- **The look reads rows' aggregates**, not rows: it runs what would be shown
  anyway, thirty statements instead of five, each a few milliseconds here.
  On a production database that is six times the statements `see` would
  run; the plan's question of when to look is still open.

## Reproducing

```bash
round-1b/.venv/Scripts/python -I round-1d/choose.py oof corpus/round-1c/learn corpus/round-1d
round-1b/.venv/Scripts/python -I round-1d/choose.py fit corpus/round-1c/learn corpus/round-1d
round-1b/.venv/Scripts/python -I round-1d/look.py pool corpus/round-1c/learn corpus/round-1d corpus/see corpus/round-1d/look --top=30 <fixture-engine>...
round-1b/.venv/Scripts/python -I round-1d/look.py pack corpus/round-1d/look <fixture-engine>...
python -I round-1d/gemini.py cut corpus/round-1d/look
JUDGE_KEY=<key> python -I round-1d/gemini.py ask corpus/round-1d/look <endpoint> <model> <judge> [<at once>]
python -I round-1d/gemini.py gather corpus/round-1d/look <judge>
JUDGE_KEY=<key> python -I round-1d/jev.py ask corpus/round-1d/look
round-1b/.venv/Scripts/python -I round-1d/look.py evaluate corpus/round-1d/look corpus/see corpus/round-1d <fixture-engine>... [--teacher=jev]
round-1b/.venv/Scripts/python -I round-1d/look.py fit corpus/round-1d/look corpus/see <fixture-engine>...
```

The pools grew in two passes, fifteen candidates and then thirty; `pool`
keeps what a pool has and `pack` packs only what no judge has seen. Sonnet
and Opus answered each packet as agents into
`corpus/round-1d/look/<judge>-<n>.json`; the answers of all six are kept in
[the data](data/look-2026-10-28/judged/). Keys were read from where they are
kept into the environment of the one command that used them, never printed
or written. Measured on Windows 11 with Python 3.13, LightGBM 4.7.0,
PostgreSQL 18.6 and MySQL 8.4.13 in Docker.
