## A bench of living databases

The 93 public databases are frozen datasets; the databases a weekly screen
is for are applications in use. Round 1m made twelve: SchemaPile-Perm schemas
that look like applications, from repositories none of round 1c's 480 came
from — loans, expenses, bike and car rental, a hotel, a shop, tuition
reimbursement, a test centre, a DVD service and others. For each, a Haiku
agent wrote a spec of what its tables are, how its columns are distributed
and how its rows grow, two or three stories planted from day 60 to 84 of
ninety, and decoys: a table imported once, a technical table, a table
mirroring another's activity. A program of round 1m made each as a
PostgreSQL database of 8 000 to 50 000 rows, read by round 1's chain like any
fixture. The specs are in [data/screen-2026-10-30/bench/](data/screen-2026-10-30/bench/).

A story is found when a panel reads what was planted — the column whose
values or money changed, or for a rate the table over a time — and shown
when that panel is drawn over a time, since a fortnight's change does not
show in ninety days' totals.

For 28 of the 29 stories the generator makes a question that shows it over
time. Round 1c's ranker places it 1st at the median for a rate (8 stories),
8th for money (7, one not made), 64th for a status's values (14): teachers
ask for rows by status, not rows over time split by status.

## What it means, and what does not follow

- The bench is twelve databases and 29 stories, a story being one or two
  panels; +6 is a large effect on a small count. Its stories, the screen's
  roles and the product judges come from one idea of what a product is
  watched for, written into prompts this round wrote; the bench measures the
  screen against that idea, not against people.
- The owner's verdict is two databases, one person. The number row was
  changed once after seeing the owner's dashboard (a draft is not the thing
  done); its other rules came from the schema reader's roles, and the bench's
  gold rows tuned it in round 1o. Haiku's gold rows are one model's opinion.
- The 93 public databases are not where a weekly screen is measured, and
  nothing here says the screen is better or worse on them.
- The number row reads whole tables: `COUNT(*)`, windows and `GROUP BY`
  over every row. Dashbin's chapter 14 allows at most 10 000 rows a table
  within 2 s and never a full scan; on the owner's databases every statement
  ran inside half a second, but the row is not yet within that rule.
