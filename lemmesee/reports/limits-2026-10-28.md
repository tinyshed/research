# What limits the look, 28 October 2026

Round 1g, as [its description](../round-1g/README.md) sets it, built and run
by the agent the same day as round 1f, after a review of round 1f by GPT:
the look taught every way at once on 93 databases by family folds, counted
on the 50 with judges that never teach, and its fives judged as sets.
Measured at research commit `670d217` with this round's files uncommitted;
raw output and the judges' answers in
[data/limits-2026-10-28/](data/limits-2026-10-28/).

## The system in one paragraph

`dashbin see` suggests the first five dashboard panels for a PostgreSQL or
MySQL database it has never seen, with no language model in production. A
generator writes candidate questions from the schema and the planner's
statistics; a ranker of 56 KB orders them (round 1c); the **look** runs the
thirty best and a model of a few kilobytes reorders them by what their
results show (rounds 1d–1f); five are chosen. Language models are only
offline teachers — Jev, DeepSeek and GLM, whose mean keep the look learns —
and blind judges that never teach — Sonnet, Gemini, and Opus on round 1d's
databases ([teaching.md](../teaching.md)).

## The data and the folds

The pools and teachers' answers of rounds 1d, 1e and 1f, as they were: 93
databases, 2 632 panels. Databases whose tables overlap by 40 % or more are
one family — Basketball men's and women's, The Simpsons' and Law and
Order's episodes, the two football leagues — and the 90 families fall into
five folds, three draws of them, so no family is taught and counted at
once. The five is the look's order with a question never twice and nothing
tuned, so nothing of a database leaks into its own five: round 1f's tuning
had used scores from looks that saw the database counted.

Judges that never teach answered every panel of 50 databases: Sonnet and
Gemini on all 50, Opus on 26 of round 1d's 28. Every measure is averaged
over the three draws per database, then over databases; each difference
from the baseline is bootstrapped over databases.

A compiler fault found in round 1f is fixed: a flag kept as an integer is
cast before PostgreSQL tests it. Talkingdata's two panels it broke stay
broken in the pools.

## What it means, and what does not follow

- **Not size, objective or context.** The look is limited by what it can
  know, not by how much it can hold: more trees memorise the teachers
  better and predict them no better on a database not seen.
- **The teachers are good enough; the student cannot follow them panel by
  panel.** Their mean would keep four of five where every panel of a pool
  is in view; the look keeps three. What separates them is what words mean
  — an identifier, a code, a technical date, a population — which 93
  databases cannot teach a model that reads only numbers.
- **A lexicon is a start, not an answer.** Nine numbers for each of 4 000
  words, written once by a language model, moved every judge the right
  way, inside chance. What the teachers use is more than nine properties
  of a word: the database's own subject, values, and how one name sits
  beside another.
- **The set is what is shown, and it tells a smaller story.** With only the
  fives in view, the gap between the look and its teachers' own five is a
  tenth or two of a panel, not one; the look beats round 1 by half a panel
  by Gemini and by a tenth by Sonnet. The per-panel counts of rounds
  1d–1f, made with all thirty candidates in view, magnified differences
  both ways; from now on a round reports the set.
- **Repeats are the set's loss, and an axis is what repeats.** Every
  look-chosen five holds a panel or so that repeats another where round
  1's rules almost never do. The rule that removes them is not one base
  and measure once — which made the five worse — but each time and
  breakdown column once: by Gemini it halves the repeats, and the look's
  five under it is the best of the round, 4.28 of five, against round 1's
  3.62. It was read from Sonnet's answers, so Sonnet has not counted it.
- **Every judge is a model**, and a page for Dmitry was left out at his
  word.

## What follows

1. **Ship the axis rule** behind the look's order, and count it on fresh
   databases by Sonnet, who has not, and by Gemini again.
2. **Bring the teachers' knowledge of words to the look** beyond nine
   numbers — a larger lexicon of roles and word pairs, or a small learned
   embedding of names, sized against the 1 MB the models may take — each
   measured by this round's folds and judged as sets. With sets as the
   measure, it is worth a tenth or two of a panel, not one.
3. Carry the question, the ranker, the look and the rule into Rust.

Sonnet answered each packet of sets as an agent, at medium effort, into
`corpus/round-1g/<set>/sonnet-<n>.json`; Gemini through the CLIProxyAPI.
Keys were read from where they are kept into the environment of the one
command that used them, never printed or written. Measured on Windows 11
with Python 3.13 and LightGBM 4.7.0.
