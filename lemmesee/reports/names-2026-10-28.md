## Why it does not help

The teachers who label the judged databases see each panel's result. The
student sees a title. Its labels on SchemaPile are what a teacher keeps
of a title, which is a different and easier thing: a title like *revenue
by month* is kept, and the student learns it. What the look misses on a
new database is what the result shows together with what the words mean:
a breakdown by a code whose values are letters, a sum over a column that
holds card numbers, a timeline of a technical date. Round 1g's errors were
of that kind. With the result in view, what the student adds about a
title the look's own numbers already hold.

So more labels by title — DeepSeek's and GLM's on the rest of the 2 886
schemas — would not have moved the look either, and the balance ran out
on labels the round did not need.

## What it cost and weighs

Jev's 85 098 panels cost $2.07. DeepSeek's 227 schemas and the values'
lexicon went on the rest of the balance; four shards' last 2 659 requests
found it empty and got no answer. GLM's batches stay queued: OpenRouter cannot cancel a
batch, only delete one that has ended, and checks a batch's estimated cost
against the balance before accepting it.

The student packs to 259 KB in 600 trees of 31 leaves, with a table of
19 333 words' keep rates beside it; the vectors would add 493 KB and the
values' lexicon 52 KB. None of it is kept.

## What follows

The next step is not more labels by title. The teachers' edge is in the
results, read with what the words mean, and only databases with data show
that; 93 are what there is. The free teachers are Gemini, through Dmitry's
own account, and Claude agents; both count today, so one would have to stop
counting to teach.
