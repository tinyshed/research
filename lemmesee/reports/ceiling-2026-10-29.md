## The count

Every five of this round is counted as round 1j counts: round 1j's family
folds and three draws, a database's five chosen with no penalties and with
each axis once, kept of five and AUC within a database, differences
bootstrapped over families. These are development comparisons on databases
already looked at, per-panel labels made with a database's pool in view, not
fives judged as sets.

From the paired differences of round 1k's first comparison, the standard
deviation of a database's difference in kept of five is 0.48 at the median
(0.25 to 0.92). For 80 % power at 5 % that sees 0.10 of a panel at 179
databases, 0.15 at 79, 0.20 at 45. Round 1g's counted 50.

**The pairwise count.** `pairwise.py` set four pairs whose difference the
per-panel count knows beside each other: the look's five against round 1's
B1 five, against itself after one click, against round 1g's best look and

the reference first and second, with the panels of each five shuffled, which

## The ideas

Each probe in [round-1k/ideas/](../round-1k/ideas/) reads what is already
here and fits nothing; the two that became numbers for the look are in the
table above.

- **The second click** (`second_click.py`): above.
- **Extensive or intensive** (`extensive.py`): every summed or averaged
  column's p5, p50 and p95 of its positive values and its share at zero or
  below, read from the round's own PostgreSQL and MySQL, 285 columns. Over
  586 sums the spread in decades meets the teachers at 0.26, the judges at
  0.18–0.28 and the look at 0.08; sums in a band under half a decade are kept
  by 5–10 % of the judges' answers, over one and a half by 24–34 %, the look
  scoring them 0.34 and 0.37. Averages run the other way.
- **The head of a name** (`head.py`): the last word the lexicon knows apart
  from the largest of the others. CreditCardNumber's `sum_ok` falls from 0.8,
  lent by credit, to number's 0.3; over sums the head's `id` meets the
  teachers at −0.20 against −0.09.
- **Fingerprints** (`fingerprints.py`): of the judged databases' 545 tables,
  15 % have a near copy (Jaccard of column names 0.9 or more) among
  SchemaPile-Perm's 164 475, of two production databases' 90 tables 1 %, of
  the pilot's under 1 %. Column names repeat where tables do not: 61 % of the
  judged databases' names and 81 % of the production databases' occur in
  SchemaPile-Perm, their head words 84 % and 100 %.
- **What a database declares** (`intent.py`): across the 94 snapshots, no
  ON UPDATE, generated column, trigger, partial index or updated row; CHECK
  in one database, enums in three, defaults in thirteen, nearly all numbers;
  the views are pg_stat_statements' but the airlines demo's. These are dumps,
  and what a living database says of itself cannot be measured on them.
- **Semantic layers** (`semantic.py`, `semantic_prior.py`): code search found
  8 324 LookML, dbt semantic-model and Malloy files; 1 521 in the 257
  repositories of permissive licences were read, 56 648 fields parsed, 6 068
  sums, 2 691 averages, 1 200 counts of distinct values. One vote a repository
  and name, the share of declarations summing a column of that name meets
  the teachers at 0.29 over sums and puts number as a head word at 2 % (111
  declarations) and weight at 5 % (3), but meets what the judges keep beyond
  the look at about zero for sums; for averages it is noisy.
- **Rules a language model writes** (`funsearch.py`): programs evolve on the
  GitTables panels and are counted once on the 93; built and checked with a
  hand rule, 0.60 within a table on the pilot and 0.55–0.57 by the judges.
  Not run.

## What it means, and what does not follow

- **The look is at its ceiling by these judges.** Judged as sets in round
  1g, the look's five with each axis once was 0.18 of a panel short of its
  teachers' own; per panel, every change of this round lands within a tenth.
  What it misses is what words mean beside a result, and every way of
  bringing that in tried here is already in what it reads.
- **The count was the blind spot.** Rounds 1h and 1j compared changes the
  count could not have seen; their negative results stand, and so do their
  positives that were within chance. From now on a round reports what its
  count can see.
- **The person is the lever.** A first click buys more than any model since
  round 1g, and a click on a panel says something of its columns that a
  model of names alone cannot. Carrying it by shared axes is not how.
- **Not established**: whether round 1c's ranker, taught on teachers' answers
  about SchemaPile-Perm schemas, saw near copies of the 15 % of judged tables
  that have them; and that `gemini-3.8-flash-high` is what answers — the
  CLIProxyAPI's log names `gemini-3.8-flash-n` as served.
