# The key model against the size of a schema, 10 November 2026

Round 1v, as [its specification](../round-1v/README.md) set it on the evening
of 9 November and wrote down, step by step, while it ran. Round 1t left the
paper's key model, `nine-mended`, 0.08 to 0.20 of F1 below four language
models, the gap recall, and each of its mends trading one shape of a key's
name for another. This round asked where the model loses, measured it by the
number of tables of a schema, asked two language models what SchemaPile's
declarations leave out, fitted the first stage anew with cues that read a name
against the whole schema, and read the result once on two sets of GitHub
schemas nobody had read. Raw output in
[data/keys-size-2026-11-10/](data/keys-size-2026-11-10/).

Every F1 is over every declared one-column key, a key right when its column
and its table are; every interval a 95 % percentile interval of 1 000
resamples of databases (seed 20261101), a difference resampling both arms
together. A precision counted with a blind judge's answers stands beside the
declared one, never in its place. Only §6's two sets are clean; every other
figure is development data and says so.

## 3. The first stage read against the whole schema

`ten` ([first.py](../round-1v/first.py)) is `nine-mended`'s recipe without its
hard negatives, which round 1t's last reading showed to cost the keys named
after their target, and with **version 10's cues** ([ten.py](../round-1v/ten.py)),
each from the names of the schema alone:

- *homes*: how many tables have the column's name as their one-column key,
  how many carry it in a key of several columns, and whether the pair's target
  is the only table whose whole key it is. Version 6 let a name point at every
  table that carries it in a key; a name four tables key by had no way to say
  which is its home, and `nine-mended` scored `codusu` into `usuario`, keyed
  `codusu`, under 0.3 in 58 tables where `1s` scored it over 0.88;
- *the target carries the name outside its key*: such a table is another
  table pointing the same way;
- *a table's name less the schema's affixes* (`fakultaet` into
  `fakultaet_table`, `rolekey` into `app_role`), how many tables hold the
  column's stem, and which table inside the column's name is the longest;
- *the column's stem less the schema's own start* (`cod` of `codusu`,
  `codper`, `codemp`).

`eleven` adds **version 11's cues for the people of a schema**, written from
the misses of `ten` on the GitHub development sets: the largest group of keys
it misses there is a column named for someone (`created_by`, `author_id`,
`owner`, `user_id`) into the schema's table of people (`users`, `profiles`). A
table is one of people by the last word of its name, so that `user_request`
is none; a column is someone's by round 1t's lexicon of actors, by a person
word among its own, or by a word for "by" at its end.

Each first stage keeps its training scores out of fold, so every second stage
is fitted on the same first stage. `graph` is round 1b's second stage with
`seven-bounded`'s bounded leaves; `wide` ([wide.py](../round-1v/wide.py)) reads
besides, for a pair, the other columns of the same name, of the same lead and
last word and those named for an actor (how many, their scores into the
pair's target, how many have it as their best), and where the pair lies
against the cut that parts the schema's columns into those that look like
keys and the rest.

## 5. What was tried and not carried

- **A schema's own valley as its threshold** ([valley.py](../round-1v/valley.py)):
  a column drawn when it passes the threshold or lies above the cut that
  parts its schema's scores in two. On `nine-mended`'s kept scores the best
  of five settings loses on eight of eleven development sets (SchemaPile's
  test 0.732 to 0.720, the fresh set 0.715 to 0.712) and gains 0.005 to
  0.010 on Spider, CTU and LiveSQLBench.
- **A name completed across its tables** ([complete.py](../round-1v/complete.py)):
  where half of the columns of one name whose best target is one table are
  drawn, the rest drawn too. On `ten--wide` it gains 0.011 on the fresh set,
  whose misses it was written from, and loses 0.001 to 0.004 on SchemaPile.
- **A sample without the columns the model itself scores as undeclared keys**
  (`ten--wide-sure`): 0.792 on the GitHub sets pooled, as `ten--wide`.
- **A teacher's key taught from all of a larger file** (`ten-taught-more`):
  its first stage was fitted and not carried further once version 11 was
  written; `eleven-taught` is taught from the same answers.
- **The second pass on the model's own scores** (`ten-sure`) and round 1t's
  reliable negatives (`ten-reliable`) were specified and not fitted: the
  teachers' answers came first and say more.

## Limits

- **The choice is one of several fits development data cannot tell apart**,
  and its second seed says so. On plain A the two seeds differ by 0.005 and
  both stand 0.05 above `nine-mended`; what is shown is the recipe, not the
  pick.
- **F1 over declared keys counts a key nobody declared as false.** §2 puts a
  third of a large SchemaPile schema's keys there, and the judge four in five
  of the choice's false keys on plain A. A model taught to draw them loses by
  this measure for that alone; the judge's precision stands beside it, on
  samples, by one judge.
- **Keys of several columns are left out of every set**, and a column of one
  drawn counts as false. The teachers name them; `eleven-taught` was not
  taught them, `ten-taught` was.
- **Versions 10 and 11 were written from the misses of development sets**:
  the fresh GitHub set, and for version 11 the second GitHub set too. Plain A
  and style 3 had been read by nothing; they come from the same pool of
  repositories as the style set and the fresh set.
- **No language model was asked on the clean sets.** The proxy's limit for
  the two fast models was spent by the teachers and the third serves the
  owner's production; how far the choice stands from them on plain A and
  style 3 is not known.
- **The teachers** are two models of one family, asked once, stopped by the
  proxy's limit: none of the 2 094 training schemas of five tables or more
  that declare no key was answered, and 599 of the 1 610 of 10 to 19 tables
  were not.
- **The judge** is one model, Claude Sonnet 5.5, of the family of Claude
  Haiku 5.5, which wrote the lexicons; on style 3 it was unsure of five
  declared keys of sixty and refused two.
- **One collector, one file a repository**: a key declared in another file
  counts as false, as round 1t found.
- **Nine words for "by"** in as many languages were written into ten.py by
  the round's agent, Claude Fable 5.2, which wrote the round's code and this
  report; they are a lexicon no measurement chose.
- **Plain A's reading was started twice.** The first run stopped at the sixth
  model on an import, having computed and written no figure; the second is
  the reading. Three times a job was lost to the machine's memory and run
  again; nothing a lost job had written was kept.
- **The harness was committed after the runs, not before them** as the
  `measure` skill asks: the owner was not there to ask during the night, and
  nothing is committed here unasked. `14ec124` holds it as it ran.

## What follows

- **For the paper**: report every F1 by the number of tables beside the
  pooled figure; say that the gap to the language models is a gap of large
  schemas, and that a large schema's declarations hold two thirds of its
  keys. Whether `eleven--wide` replaces `nine-mended` as its model is the
  owner's to decide; the reading that would be the paper's is plain A's
  +0.055 and style 3's +0.011.
- **The reader**: versions 10 and 11 are name cues of the first stage; the
  wide second stage is not shown to add anything, so `eleven--graph`'s recipe
  needs no new stage in Rust. It was not read on the clean sets; `ten--graph`

- **The language models on plain A and style 3**, under round 1s's protocol,
  when the owner approves the requests.
- **Plain B stays clean**, 718 schemas frozen by hash, for the next model.
- **Large schemas are still where the model loses**: 0.651 at 40 tables or

  is not there, a column of a key of several columns — has to be kept out,

  the declared one before a taught model can be chosen.
- **Two seeds a fit** from here on.
