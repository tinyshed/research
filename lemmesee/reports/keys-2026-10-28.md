Round 1b's second pass, as [its specification](../round-1b/README.md#the-second-pass-on-keys)
sets it: the key model of [the first report](declarations-2026-10-27.md)
developed on the validation split alone — letters, one target a column,
bigger trees, a stage over the schema's graph, training on the graphics
card — then measured once on the test and on 120 hard cases. Measured at
research commit `1524905`; raw output in [data/keys-2026-10-28/](data/keys-2026-10-28/).
**It supersedes the first report's key model**; that report's data, rules
and closed lists stand.

## The second pass, on validation

The rules on the validation split: precision 0.809, recall 0.281, F1 0.417.
Each threshold is the best F1 on validation itself, the same for every
variant, so these compare variants and are not the result; the result is
the test below. Times are one run each on the machine below.

| Variant | F1, one target a column | Keys at the rules' precision | F1 on novel schemas | Trees | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the first report's trees, 200 of 15 leaves | 0.711 | 31 % | 0.704 | 200 | 59 s |
| with letters | 0.706 | 24 % | 0.700 | 200 | 92 s |
| with letters, 31 leaves, stopped on validation | 0.770 | 64 % | 0.740 | 1 961 | 794 s |
| with letters, 63 leaves, stopped on validation | 0.775 | 65 % | 0.745 | 1 091 | 503 s |
| the same in `gpu.py`, LightGBM on 16 threads | 0.768 | 63 % | — | 1 174 | 321 s |
| the same in `gpu.py`, XGBoost on the card | 0.761 | 60 % | — | 829 | 54 s |

Letters alone did not help 200 small trees; they helped as soon as the
trees could use them. One target a column added 0.06 to the first report's
model and almost nothing to the bigger ones.

**The graph stage**, five first models each fitted without the fold of
repositories it scores, then a second model on nineteen features of those
scores over the schema:

| First stage | First alone: F1 | at the rules' precision | With the graph: F1 | at the rules' precision | On complete schemas, first → graph |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LightGBM, 550 trees of 63 leaves | 0.761 | 61 % | 0.766 | 68 % | 0.801 → 0.806 |
| XGBoost on the card, 829 trees | 0.761 | 62 % | 0.762 | 67 % | 0.798 → 0.802 |

The second model leaned most on the first's score, on how far a column's
best target leads its next, on how well columns ending in the same word
scored elsewhere in the schema, and on the other arrows into the same
table. The configuration in the specification — the card's first stage, the
graph, one target a column — was fixed from these before the test was read.

## The test, once

The threshold, the best F1 on validation, was 0.832. The interval is of the
model's F1 less the rules', from 1 000 resamples of test repositories.

| Test schemas | Pairs | Keys | Rules: P | R | F1 | Model: P | R | F1 | Area | Keys at the rules' precision | Interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 784 893 | 11 561 | 0.769 | 0.310 | 0.442 | 0.728 | 0.827 | **0.774** | 0.796 | 79 % | +0.294 to +0.369 |
| novel | 447 487 | 8 954 | 0.782 | 0.313 | 0.447 | 0.712 | 0.802 | **0.755** | 0.776 | 72 % | +0.258 to +0.354 |
| complete | 552 644 | 9 409 | 1.000 | 0.261 | 0.414 | 0.794 | 0.847 | **0.819** | 0.850 | — | +0.362 to +0.445 |

Against the first report's best — F1 0.660 on all the test schemas, 0.652 on
the novel, and 46 % of the keys at the rules' precision — the second pass
gains 0.10 to 0.11 of F1 and finds the keys at the rules' precision 1.7
times as often. **Complete** schemas are those where every key the rules
guess by name is declared: their labels miss no obvious key, which makes the
rules perfectly precise there by construction and the column of keys at
their precision meaningless.

## The hard cases

120 schemas, 753 keys, written by three Claude Haiku agents on 28 October,
each to a style ([round 1b](../round-1b/README.md#the-second-pass-on-keys)).
Never trained on nor used to choose; measured with the test's threshold. A
decoy is a column that looks like a key and is not; only the 60 whose type
meets some table's key can be taken for one.

| Style | Keys | Rules: P | R | F1 | Model: P | R | F1 | Decoys | taken by the rules | by the model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 753 | 1.000 | 0.373 | 0.544 | 0.959 | 0.805 | **0.875** | 60 | 0 | 3 |
| legacy and abbreviations | 229 | 1.000 | 0.083 | 0.153 | 0.991 | 0.926 | **0.957** | 54 | 0 | 2 |
| other languages | 170 | 1.000 | 0.641 | 0.781 | 1.000 | 0.971 | **0.985** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| keys named by role | 354 | 1.000 | 0.432 | 0.604 | 0.905 | 0.647 | **0.755** | 6 | 0 | 1 |

The rules are never wrong here because the agents declared every relation,
so a key named for its table is always one. The model reads `CUST_ID`,
`cust_no` and `polzovatel_id` where the rules cannot; it still misses a
third of the keys named by role — `assignee_id`, `blocked_id` — where only
the schema's shape says which table they mean.

## What the numbers cannot say

- **Every label is a declaration.** Of the model's false arrows on
  validation, 17 to 19 % were named exactly for their target, and are most
  likely keys their schema never declared; the complete schemas, at F1
  0.819, are the nearest this corpus comes to clean labels.
- **The hard cases were written by a language model**, from prompts that
  named the patterns to use; real legacy and transliterated schemas are
  messier. They show where the rules fail, not how well the model does in
  the wild.
- **One run each.** LightGBM's threads made runs of the same configuration
  differ by about 0.01 of F1 in development; the test was read once, and a
  second reading could move its last digit.

## What follows

- **Carry the two stages into Rust**: the features computed the same way as
  in Python — tokens, letter pieces, the hashing scikit-learn uses — checked
  pair by pair against Python's scores, the trees in the compact format, and
  the memory and the time a column measured in the container. A first cut of
  each column's targets by the cheap name score keeps a schema of a hundred
  tables inside chapter 14's millisecond.
- **Into `roles.rs`** as evidence of its own kind, below a declaration, a
  guessed join still flagged.
- **Past 0.8: keys from statistics.** A column whose distinct values fit
  within a table's rows, and whose range fits within its key's, is likely
  pointing there; PostgreSQL's catalog says both without reading a row. The
  next round learns that from databases with data, BIRD's among them.
